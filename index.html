Solving a murder with SQL

A murder happened in SQL City on Jan. 15, 2018, and the crime report was lost. Starting from those three facts, I used SQL to trace the killer and the person behind the crime.

The investigation trail

Each query used what the previous one revealed. Blue marks the two breakthroughs.

  1. Recover the crime scene report

    Filtered the report table by city (SQL City), type (murder) and date (Jan. 15, 2018) to find the two witnesses.

  2. Find witness one, Annabel

    Matched a name pattern on Franklin Ave in the person table, then joined to the interview table for her transcript.

  3. Follow the gym lead

    Annabel recognized the killer from her gym. I joined gym membership and check-in records and kept gold members whose visit times overlapped hers.

  4. Read witness two’s interview

    Found the witness in the last house on Northwestern Dr by sorting house numbers in descending order and taking the top row.

  5. Identify the killer

    Combined the gold membership, a membership ID pattern and the plate number from the driver’s license table, using a CTE and joins. This pointed to Jeremy Bowers, confirmed against the solution table.

  6. Read the killer’s interview

    With the killer found, his own interview transcript revealed that someone else had hired him.

  7. Unmask the mastermind

    Filtered Facebook check-ins for people who attended the SQL Symphony Concert three times in December 2017, then matched them to the described driver’s license details. Miranda Priestly was confirmed in the solution table.

What this shows

01

Start small, then chain

Begin with the few known facts and let every result shape the next query.

02

Join across sources

The answer lived across tables. Joins connected people, interviews, memberships, licenses and events.

03

Filter precisely

Pattern matching, date ranges, time overlaps and HAVING counts turned vague clues into a short list.

04

Verify every conclusion

Each finding was checked against the solution table before moving on.
